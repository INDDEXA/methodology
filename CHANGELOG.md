## What changed in the published text

### Document framing

- V1.1 adds an explicit document title (`V1.1 Research Methodology (Revised)`), a one-line version summary and a "Prepared by INDDEXA Research Department" attribution. The V1.0 body begins directly at "Research method".
- The opening sentence changes from "This is Version 1.0 of the INDDEXA research methodology" to "This is Version 1.1, revised edition, of the INDDEXA research methodology".
- V1.0 says "Later versions will be published at their own versioned URL"; V1.1 says "Revisions keep the same canonical page; a substantive upgrade is published at its own versioned URL". The links to the Editorial Policy and Corrections Policy are retained in both.

### Section 1 — Purpose and scope

- V1.1 adds a four-element test for an information increment: what is new relative to the baseline, what evidence supports it, what reproducible method produced it, and what its scope and uncertainties are. It states that if any one cannot be stated, no increment is claimed.

### Section 3 — Baseline of existing information

- V1.1 adds that the baseline is fixed when the study is commissioned, not written after the findings are in, and that a missing baseline is a research defect, not a writing flaw.

### Section 4 — What counts as a contribution

- V1.1 adds to "Independent verification" that a negative result is a legitimate outcome of verification.
- V1.1 adds a paragraph listing optional techniques for surfacing an increment: re-examining a question with a different time window, region or source type; tracing and recalculating a repeatedly cited figure; connecting public items into a supported proposition; rebuilding a dataset under one definition; testing an assumed premise and reporting honestly; and tracking how a commitment was met or changed over time. It states that the contribution is judged by the six definitions and by section 5, not by the technique itself.
- The six contribution types, their priority order, and the list of what does not count as a contribution are unchanged.

### Section 5 — How a study is carried out

- V1.1 adds to Step 3 that where an approach from section 4 is useful, it is chosen and written into the research plan at that stage, not added after the conclusions are known.
- V1.1 rewrites Step 5 to "set out four things" and cross-references the four elements described in section 1.

### Section 6 — Sources and grades of evidence

- V1.1 adds a paragraph on negative results and findings of "not found": conclusions are limited to the sources listed and the scope examined; sources that should have been checked but were not are recorded with the reasons; and qualifying language in the title, summary, key findings and body must be consistent.

### Section 10 — Assistance from AI

- V1.1 adds a paragraph stating that AI may carry out a method and judgements designed by people; that people must design the method, take responsibility for the conclusions and trace every fact back to its source; and that real interviews, field tests, access to paid databases, sensitive commercial judgement and final approval are carried out by people.

### Section 11 — Updates, versions and corrections

- V1.1 adds the distinction between a revision and an upgrade: this edition is described as a **revision** that clarifies wording and adds method examples and public-facing explanation without adding a public section, changing a public commitment, or altering a criterion or value, so it keeps the same canonical page. A later upgrade that adds a public section or changes the scope of the public commitment would receive a new version number and its own canonical URL.
- The wording changes from "Earlier versions are retained internally" (V1.0) to "Earlier versions are retained" (V1.1).

### Section 12 — Limits of this version

- The opening sentence changes from "Version 1.0 is the first complete public statement…" to "Version 1.1 is the first complete public statement…".
- V1.1 adds a paragraph describing this revised edition as doing no more than clarify wording and add public-facing explanation: the four-element test for an information increment, optional techniques, negative results and qualifying language, evidence grades as working labels, the boundaries of AI assistance, and the difference in URL handling between a revision and an upgrade. It states that the text does not change the scope of the public commitment, add or remove a contribution type, or publish any internal score or threshold.
- The closing sentence adds an explicit link: this version "will remain available for reference at [V1.0](/methodology/V1.0/)".

### Version history block

- The V1.0 body has no version-history block. V1.1 adds one with two entries: "V1.0 — 2026-11-19" (linked to https://inddexa.com/methodology/V1.0/) and "V1.0, Revised — 2026-11-22", listing the clarifications and noting no new sections, no new values and no new canonical URL. It closes with the italic attribution "*INDDEXA Research Department*".

### Typographic notes

- Where V1.0 uses typographic (curly) quotation marks around "information increment" in section 1, V1.1 uses straight double quotation marks.
- Body heading levels differ: V1.0 uses `##` for sections, V1.1 uses `###` for sections.

## What did not change

- The five-step structure of section 5 (fix the question, complete the baseline, design the method, collect evidence and keep a record, state the increment); two of the steps were expanded as noted above.
- The six contribution types in section 4, in the same priority order.
- The three internal evidence grades in section 6, described in both versions as working labels rather than a public accreditation.
- The six status labels for material claims in section 7: confirmed; partly confirmed; not yet verified; disputed or in conflict across sources; inference drawn from evidence; opinion.
- The list of usual elements of a published piece in section 8.
- The publication gate and independent-review requirements in section 9, including the requirement of a human decision to publish.
- The section 10 rule that AI tools may not invent interviews, data, tests, citations or identities; that a model output is not an external source; and that a model does not review its own draft or take the decision to publish.
- The statement in both versions that internal working scores and trial thresholds are used as provisional management tools and are not published as standards.
